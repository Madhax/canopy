# 04 · Flow Policies — failure correctness for a running fleet

> **Status:** Proposed 2026-08-16 · **Reads with:** `README.md`, `../standing-orgs.md` (trigger semantics this completes), `../organizations/04-scheduling-and-throttles.md` §9.4 (the admission boundary this closes — CAP-D10), `../../execution/engine.md`, `../../actuation/phase3-debts.md` (F1 residual), `../../canopy-inc.md` §4.2 (foundry, the branch-drift customer)

## 0. The problem

Three places where the flow, left alone for a day, silently does the wrong thing: trigger-fired intents bypass the capacity governor (CAP-D10 — the one ungoverned intake is the one an unattended fleet lives on); a trigger-born intent that *fails* is never retried (the fire ledger's at-most-once is correct for dedup and wrong as a permanent verdict); and long-lived engagement branches drift against a `main` the operator merges into daily, discovering conflicts at the worst moment (PR assembly). Plus the standing debt that converts directly into attention: salaries are pre-cache-reality placeholders, and every mis-sized meter is a hard-stop the envelope or the operator must absorb.

## 1. Trigger admission — closing CAP-D10

**FL-1** `TriggerScheduler._fire` consults the governor *before* creating anything: `Scheduler.admit_trigger(team)` (the `admit_cadence` twin — org weekly ceiling, run state, reserve/contention posture). On `hold`: **no fire row is written and the cursor does not advance past the candidate** — the event simply remains unfired and is retried next pass, preserving both invariants of `standing-orgs.md` §7 (at-most-once via same-tx fire row + intent; never-consume for unfired events). A deduped `trigger.skipped reason=capacity|budget|paused` receipt lands in activity (the cadence-skip precedent), so a morning of held triggers is one brief line, not silence. **FL-2** Admission order is preserved fairness: held candidates re-enter through the same `maxPerPass` throat — no catch-up burst on resume beyond the existing cap.

## 2. Refire — failure is a verdict with an appeal window

**FL-3** The fire ledger row gains `outcome` (`open → completed | failed | cancelled`), written when the born intent reaches a terminal state. **FL-4** Auto-refire: a `failed` outcome (infrastructure/hard-stop/`failed` terminal — not `rejected`-then-abandoned judgments, not operator `cancelled`) makes the external key eligible again after `refireAfterH` (default 24), at most `maxRefires` (default 2) — each refire a *new* fire row chained to the last (`refire_of`), so the at-most-once ledger becomes an at-most-N ledger with full provenance. **FL-5 — Two strikes parks.** After `maxRefires` failures the key parks: a `refire-strikes` anomaly in the brief with the transcript links, and the trigger will not touch that key again without an explicit operator re-arm (one click, audited). This is simultaneously the recovery policy and a resource-burn defense — a poisoned issue gets two bounded attempts, never a nightly grind (`05` §3 leans on this). **FL-6** Operator re-arm exists independently of strikes (re-run a key at will, superseding its chain), for the "the fix landed, try the issue again" morning.

## 3. Branch freshness — the rebase policy

**FL-7** PR assembly and the pr-create executor gain a **freshness gate**: if the engagement branch's merge-base is stale against `main` (behind by more than `[flow] max_base_age_days = 7`, or a test-merge detects conflicts), assembly refuses and the platform opens a **rebase step** instead: a mechanical `git rebase main` in the worktree. **FL-8** Clean rebase → proceed; QA re-verification is re-requested only if rebased-over commits touch paths the suite covers (conservative default: any conflict ⇒ re-verify; clean ⇒ optional). **FL-9** Conflicted rebase → the conflict is the engineer's work, not the platform's: an anchored note lands in the owning session ("rebase conflicts in …; resolve in your worktree") at the next boundary — the agent has the tools, the workspace, and the context. **FL-10** The gate runs *before* the operator ever sees the PR, so ratification is always of a mergeable artifact; "mergeable at brief time" is part of the PR card's receipt. Foundry's WIP-1 discipline (`canopy-inc.md` §4.2) keeps the blast radius small; docs pods rarely conflict; the policy exists for the day either assumption fails.

## 4. Salary calibration — retiring the F1 residual mechanically

**FL-11 — Calibration is a report, not a vibe.** A `salary-calibration` view over the receipts corpus: per role (and per team-role), the distribution of per-assignment burn — cache-aware, per the F1 fix — over the trailing window: p50, p90, n, and the *hard-stop and top-up rates* at current allowances. **FL-12** The report proposes allowances: `p90 × 1.25`, warn at 80% — rendered as a diff against catalog defaults, shippable as a catalog PR (forge's lane once it exists; operator-applied before then). **FL-13 — The bootstrap rule:** until a role has `n ≥ 5` real assignments in the corpus, its team either runs `planReview.mode: always` (a human sees the allowances at every dispatch) or carries envelope auto-top-up as the shock absorber — mis-calibration is absorbed by policy while the data accrues, instead of by 3am hard-stops. **FL-14** Meter currency stays token-denominated in v1 (the smaller blast radius; the cost-weighted re-decision remains open in `phase3-debts.md`), but the report displays both tokens and estimated USD so the eventual re-decision is made against evidence.

## 5. Open questions

1. Should `rejected`-terminal trigger intents ever auto-refire? Leaning no — a manager's rejection is a judgment, and judgments don't retry themselves; the operator re-arm covers the exceptions.
2. Freshness-gate thresholds per team kind (docs vs. code)? Start uniform; let the anomaly data argue.
3. Does the rebase step burn the engineer's meter? Leaning yes (it is that assignment's work — honest costs), with the conflict-free platform rebase free (no session, no tokens).
4. Per-org calibration overrides vs. catalog-global proposals — where does a team whose burn profile diverges from its role's norm get its number? Likely team-level extension first, catalog PR when the divergence proves general.
